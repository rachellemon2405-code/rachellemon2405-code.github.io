The Environmental Cost of Data Centers

Data centers are the buildings that store and process the information behind everything we do online, from streaming video to using AI tools. They run around the clock, and that constant operation requires huge amounts of electricity and water.

Much of that electricity still comes from fossil fuels, which adds to greenhouse gas emissions. Data centers also use large amounts of water to cool their equipment, which can strain local water supplies, especially in dry regions. As demand for cloud computing and AI grows, so does concern about these impacts. Communities near large data centers can also face added strain on their local power grids, water supply, and local wilflide.
